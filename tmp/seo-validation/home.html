Solar Costs

What Does Solar
Actually Cost?

A residential solar installation in Texas often lands between $18,000 and $28,000 before incentives. The final number depends on system size, equipment, electrical scope, and how much energy the home actually uses.

If you are trying to decide whether solar is worth it, start with the numbers that matter most: total project cost, incentives, expected production, and realistic payback.

30%

Federal Tax Credit

The federal Investment Tax Credit lets you claim 30% of your total solar cost against your federal taxes. On a $22,000 system, that's $6,600 back.

$$$

Texas Utility Context

Savings depend on your actual utility, electric rate, usage pattern, and whether the system is sized to offset the right amount of annual consumption.

$0

Property Tax Increase

Solar panels can increase your home's value, but Texas law exempts that added value from property taxes. Your tax bill does not automatically go up just because the home appraises higher.

Realistic Payback

Most homeowners still see solar break even in roughly 7–9 years.

The strongest quotes clearly separate contract cost, incentives, and expected production so you can compare options without guessing where the savings are supposed to come from.
