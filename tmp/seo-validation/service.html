Overview

Your roof is already collecting energy — you’re just not using it.

Right now, that sunlight turns into heat your AC has to fight. Solar panels convert it into electricity instead. Add a battery, and you have backup power with no generator fuel, no noise, no maintenance. Panels cost 70% less than a decade ago, the federal tax credit covers 30%, and Texas’s deregulated market means you actually benefit from what you produce. We design your system around your real electric bills — not a generic estimate — so the numbers stay honest.

What a good solar and battery installations scope should cover

Custom system design based on your actual usage
Panel count, battery size, and inverter capacity are all sized to match how your household actually uses energy throughout the year. No cookie-cutter proposals.
30% federal tax credit on total installed cost
The ITC covers 30% of everything — panels, batteries, inverter, wiring, labor, and permitting. Dollar-for-dollar credit on your federal taxes.
Battery backup keeps critical systems running
When the grid drops, your battery kicks in automatically — fridge, lights, internet, medical equipment. No transfer switch to flip, no generator to start.

How solar and battery installations should be approached

Step 1
Pull your utility data, run a shade analysis, and assess roof condition
We analyze 12 months of usage to size the system to your actual consumption — not a generic estimate. A shade study maps shadow patterns from trees, chimneys, and neighboring structures across every hour and season. Roof age, material, orientation, pitch, and structural condition all have to check out before we design anything.
Step 2
Custom system design, financing options, permits, and utility interconnection
Your proposal includes panel count, inverter type, battery sizing (if applicable), projected savings, and payback timeline with and without the 30% federal tax credit. We handle building permits, electrical permits, and the utility interconnection application — that’s typically 2–4 weeks of paperwork you never have to touch.
Step 3
Install in 1–2 days, pass city inspection, and activate with live monitoring
Panels, racking, inverter, and battery go on your roof and wall. After city inspection and utility permission to operate (PTO), the system goes live. We set up your monitoring app so you see production in real time — and we verify the first week’s output against the design model to make sure everything performs as promised.

Solar and Battery Installations questions

How much does a solar system cost in Texas?
A typical residential system runs $15,000–$30,000 before the 30% tax credit. After the credit, most systems cost $10,500–$21,000. Battery adds $8,000–$15,000 depending on capacity. We break it all down in your proposal.
Do I need a new roof before solar?
If your roof has less than 10 years of life left, yes — it should be discussed first. Putting panels on a roof that needs replacing in 5 years means paying for detach-and-reset later. We’ll be honest about the timing.
