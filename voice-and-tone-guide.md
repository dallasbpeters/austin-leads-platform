# Voice & Tone Guide — Texas Solar and Roofing Pros

Based on analysis of Longhorn Solar and Ja-Mar Roofing — two established Texas competitors — plus what we want to sound like versus what we want to avoid.

---

## Who we're talking to

Texas homeowners who are dealing with a roof problem, thinking about solar, or both. They're not browsing for fun. Something happened — a storm, a high electric bill, an insurance letter, a neighbor's new panels — and now they're trying to figure out what to do next. They want someone who knows what they're talking about, tells the truth, and doesn't waste their time.

---

## What we sound like

We sound like someone who does this work every day and is used to explaining it clearly. Not a brochure. Not a blog post trying to rank. A person sitting across the table from you who happens to know a lot about roofs and solar.

**Core voice traits:**

- **Direct.** Say what it is. Don't dance around it.
- **Knowledgeable but not showing off.** Use real numbers and real terms, but explain them when they matter.
- **Calm.** No urgency. No countdown timers. No "act now." If the work is good, people will call.
- **Honest about limitations.** If solar doesn't pencil out, say so. If a roof has 5 more years in it, say that too.
- **Texas-grounded.** References to real places, real weather, real utility companies. Not generic "homeowner" copy.

---

## Sentence-level patterns that work

These patterns show up in the best competitor copy and feel right for our audience:

| Pattern                                   | Example                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lead with the situation, not the product  | "After a hailstorm passes through, the damage isn't always obvious from the ground."                  |
| Name the real thing, not the category     | "Class 4 impact-rated shingles" not "premium roofing materials"                                       |
| Explain what happens next, not what we do | "We check the electrical panel, inspect the roof, measure for shade, and confirm everything's ready." |
| Specifics over adjectives                 | "Over 2,300 installations across Texas" not "thousands of happy customers"                            |
| Short sentences after long ones           | Break up rhythm. Don't let paragraphs drone.                                                          |
| Let the reader decide                     | "You'll see the numbers side by side" not "You'll love the savings"                                   |

## Headline rules

Headlines should describe what's below them, not try to be clever.

**Good headlines sound like a chapter title, not a billboard:**

- "Roofing work starts with condition, not assumptions"
- "What a site assessment actually covers"
- "How the insurance claim process works in Texas"
- "Storm damage isn't always visible from the ground"

**Bad headlines try too hard:**

- "Your roof. Your call." _(vague, sounds like a tagline)_
- "We start with your roof, not a pitch deck" _(forced cleverness)_
- "The roof tells us what it needs" _(anthropomorphized roof = weird)_
- "Roofing services for every situation" _(generic filler)_

**The test:** If someone skimming the page reads only the headlines, do they know what the page covers? If not, rewrite them.

## Words and phrases to never use

These make copy sound like it was generated or written by a marketing team that's never been on a roof:

- **"The roof"** — nobody talks like this. Say "your roof" or just "it."
- **"Game-changer"**
- **"No-brainer"**
- **"Don't miss out"**
- **"Act now" / "Limited time"** — unless it's actually a dated incentive with a real expiration
- **"We're passionate about..."**
- **"Solutions" / "Roofing solutions"** — say what you actually do
- **"Best-in-class"**
- **"Turnkey" / "Seamless"** — fine internally, sounds like a brochure externally
- **"Value proposition"**
- **"At [Company], we believe..."**
- **"Sales pitch"** — don't reference it, even to say you don't have one
- **"This is where [Company] shines!"** — self-congratulatory
- **"One-of-a-kind"** — unverifiable, sounds like hype
- **"Don't worry"** — patronizing
- Any phrase with an exclamation point at the end of a factual statement

---

## What Longhorn Solar does well (steal this)

- **Specific proof points:** "Over 2,300 installations," "average over 7 years with the company," "Master Solar Electrician"
- **"No subcontractors" framing:** Tells you what they DON'T do, which builds more trust than listing what they do
- **Process transparency:** Five clear steps, each with a name and explanation. Reader knows exactly what happens.
- **Honest upfront:** "even if it means solar isn't right for you" — that line does more work than any testimonial

---

## What Longhorn Solar does poorly (avoid this)

- **"This is where it all starts!"** — exclamation marks on factual statements
- **"This is where Longhorn Solar shines!"** — self-congratulatory
- **"Our PV Design Tool, SolarCalc, is one-of-a-kind. No other solar company has anything like it."** — unverifiable, sounds like hype
- **"We are happy to answer as many questions as you can come up with"** — filler, no one doubts they can ask questions
- **"Huge part of our value proposition"** — internal business jargon leaking into customer-facing copy

---

## What Ja-Mar does well (steal this)

- **50+ years, with the number front and center.** Longevity is the proof. They don't oversell it — they just say it.
- **Credential specificity:** "GAF Master Elite — only 2% of roofers in North America." That stat does the selling.
- **FAQ answers that teach:** Their FAQ section gives real, specific answers — code references (ICC), rating systems (UL 2218 Class 4), insurance discount percentages (35%). This is the kind of content that ranks and builds trust.
- **Testimonials name the rep:** "I worked with Ken," "Drew made us feel comfortable," "Thomas Alden came out." Names make the company feel real.
- **"Defer to Close" program:** Named clearly, explained in one paragraph, positioned for a specific audience (sellers). Good example of a feature explained without hype.

---

## What Ja-Mar does poorly (avoid this)

- **SEO-stuffed sentences:** "The professional Austin roofers at Ja-Mar Roofing & Sheet Metal understand how important it is to keep your roof in good condition" — reads like it was written for Google, not a person
- **"Mother Nature does not discriminate"** — forced metaphor
- **"call your Austin Roofers today"** — keyword-stuffed CTA
- **Generic openers:** "Your house is one of the biggest and most important purchases you'll make in your lifetime" — reader already knows this
- **Award sections that go on too long:** The GAF credential is strong. Repeating it three different ways dilutes it.

---

## Formatting rules for site copy

- **No exclamation marks** in body copy unless quoting a real person
- **One CTA per section.** Don't stack "Get a Quote" and "Call Now" and "Schedule Today" in the same block.
- **Paragraphs stay under 4 sentences.** If it's longer, break it up or cut it.
- **Headlines are lowercase except first word and proper nouns.** Not Title Case.
- **Use numbers, not words,** for anything above ten. Below ten, use words unless it's a stat (e.g., "5 peak sun hours").
- **Bold sparingly.** If everything is bold, nothing is.
- **No em dashes in headlines.** Fine in body copy.

---

## The quick gut check

Before publishing any page copy, ask:

1. Would a roofer say this to a homeowner standing in their driveway? If not, rewrite it.
2. Does every paragraph tell the reader something they didn't already know? If not, cut it.
3. Could a competitor's site say the exact same thing with their name swapped in? If yes, it's too generic.
4. Is there a specific number, place, code, or timeline anywhere on this page? If not, add one.
