## 1. The short version

The question you asked was Jobber or QuoteIQ. The honest answer is that neither one is your actual problem. Your crew shares a single Jobber login to keep the bill down, and almost everything that frustrates them traces back to that one decision.

Once we started digging, three things came clear.

**First, the shared login is probably what's breaking the app.** Your crew says the Jobber app is unusable. We read the public reviews, and the complaints that match theirs — getting kicked offline, clock-ins that don't stick, schedules that never update — are exactly what happens when several phones share one account. Jobber's own terms say accounts are for one person only.

**Second, QuoteIQ can't do what your website needs.** You've had a system built that files invoices and customers into Jobber automatically. QuoteIQ has no way to accept that. It can send information out; it can't take information in. That automation would go back to being somebody's job.

**Third, there are better options than QuoteIQ on your shortlist.** Zoho Field Service fits your existing setup and its pricing would end the login problem outright — but its phone app is the worst of everything we looked at, which is a serious problem given what your crew is already telling you. JobNimbus is built for roofers, has the best-rated field app of the group, and costs roughly three times what you'd pay Jobber.

### What we recommend

> If the complaints stop, you've solved it for the price of a decent tool. If they don't, you'll have a tested reason to switch and clean per-person data to take with you — and the option to look at should be **JobNimbus**, not QuoteIQ.
>
> Either way, we'd hold off on QuoteIQ. The automation you've already paid for has nowhere to land there.

## 2. Start here: the shared login is costing you more than it saves

You share Jobber logins to avoid paying per person. That's an understandable reaction to Jobber's pricing — $29 a month per head adds up fast. But it's buying you a $348-a-year saving in exchange for four real costs.

- **You've lost your audit trail.** Every quote, price change, and deleted job carries the same name. If a number gets changed, nobody can tell you who changed it.
- **Time and GPS tracking mean nothing.** Both key off the user. One login shared six ways can't tell you who was where.
- **Offboarding is a fire drill.** When someone leaves, the password has to change for everyone. In practice it usually doesn't.
- **The account itself is at risk.** Jobber's terms of service, Section 20, say plainly: _"User accounts are intended for one user only and are not to be shared by multiple users."_ It's a small risk, but the downside is your whole operation stopping.

### And it's probably why the app doesn't work

Your crew says the Jobber app is bad enough that they won't use it. We took that seriously and went to the app stores rather than taking anyone's word for it.

**Table 1 — What Jobber users complain about**

| Complaint                   | What it sounds like                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Getting kicked offline      | "I have to close the app and open back up multiple times a day because I keep going _offline_."                                              |
| Time clock failing silently | The app "will tell you it's clocked you in when it has not" — "not a very reliable application for something as important as tracking time." |
| Schedules not updating      | "Our technician's schedules are not updating live as I'm updating their schedules, and it is causing critical notes to be missed."           |

Read those three together. Dropping offline, clock-ins that don't stick, schedule changes that never arrive — those are all **session** problems. They're what happens when one account is signed in on six phones and every device keeps knocking the others out. A crew living with that would reasonably conclude the app is broken.

> ### The most important finding in this report
>
> **Your crew is right that it doesn't work. They may be wrong about why.**
>
> We can't prove this from outside your account — it's a hypothesis. But it fits the symptoms closely, and it's cheap to test. The test costs less than one day of the migration it might save you.
>
> And if we're wrong, you still end up compliant, with a real audit trail and per-person time data. There's no version of this where the money is wasted.

### The 30-day test

1. **Buy nine more Jobber seats.** About $261/month on your current Core plan. Every crew member gets their own login.
2. **Change the shared password.** Otherwise the old habit quietly continues and the test tells you nothing.
3. **Ask the crew again after 30 days.** Same question, same people. Write down what they say this time.

If the complaints stop, you're done — and you got your audit trail and time tracking back in the bargain. If they continue, you've earned something more valuable than an opinion: a tested reason to switch, and clean per-person history to hand whoever you move to.

**How we reasoned through this**

1. We didn't dismiss the complaint. Field staff refusing to use software is a real operational failure whatever the cause, and Jobber's Android reviews do contain genuine reports of offline and clock-in trouble.
2. We checked whether the alternatives are actually better before assuming it. On the phone apps, two of them aren't — see the next section.
3. We looked for a cause that explains the whole pattern rather than each complaint separately. Multi-device account sharing explains all three at once.
4. We compared the two experiments by what they cost. Proper seats for a month is about $261 and reversible. A migration is 45 to 75 hours of development and isn't. When the cheap test can rule out the expensive one, run the cheap test first.

## 4. Cost

These five products charge in three completely different ways, and that's what makes them hard to compare.

- **Per person.** Jobber and JobNimbus. A base price, plus a fee for every head. This is the model that pushed you into sharing logins.
- **Per bundle of seats.** QuoteIQ. Each plan includes a set number of people; adding one costs nothing until you outgrow the plan, then you jump a tier.
- **Per job.** Zoho FSM. You pay for appointments per month, and the user count is nearly unlimited — up to 200 people on any paid plan.

## 5. The Zoho question — you're already living in Zoho

This came up when we looked through your platform's code, and it changes the shape of the decision. You aren't a Jobber company deciding whether to try something else. You're a Zoho company that also uses Jobber.

**Table 4 — Zoho already running in your platform**

| Zoho product   | What it does for you today                                                              |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Zoho CRM       | Holds your contacts. Your quoting screen searches them directly as you type.            |
| Zoho Sheet     | Every quote and every Christmas-lights invoice lands in a spreadsheet automatically.    |
| Zoho WorkDrive | Stores documents, one folder per client.                                                |
| Zoho sign-in   | Your team signs into the admin site with their Zoho accounts. They already have logins. |

Read that list and Jobber starts to look like the outlier rather than the foundation. Your quotes already live in Zoho. Your contacts already live in Zoho. Your team already has Zoho accounts.

### What Zoho Field Service would add

- **Pricing by job, not by head.** Paid plans support up to 200 users. Your whole crew gets individual logins for no extra charge — the shared-login problem disappears rather than being paid off.
- **A real two-way API.** REST, with create, read, update and delete, included on every plan including the free one. Unlike QuoteIQ, your website could keep filing invoices and customers automatically.
- **Native sync with the CRM you already use.** Contacts, accounts and products sync both directions with Zoho CRM through an official extension.
- **Custom fields and webhooks** from the Standard plan up — things you're currently doing by hand on Jobber's Core.
- **Fewer vendors.** One bill, one support line, one login system.

### And the reason we're not recommending it yet

> **The catch: Zoho FSM's field app is the weakest of everything we looked at — and your problem is a field app.**
>
> 2.7 stars on iPhone, 3.9 on Android. The written complaints are about exactly the things that would make a crew give up: too many taps to finish a job, an interface that "feels incomplete", no search on the work-order list.
>
> Moving a crew that already refuses one app onto a worse-rated one is a bad bet, however good the pricing is. The ratings come from small samples, so this is a caution rather than a verdict — but it needs testing before it needs a contract.

There's also the appointment-volume question. The entry price covers 60 appointments a month. We don't know your monthly job count, and if it's several hundred the price could land well above the chart. That's the first thing to find out.

**Our suggestion on Zoho:** don't rule it out, but don't commit to it either. Zoho FSM has a free tier that supports up to 20 users. Put two or three people on it for two weeks alongside Jobber and see what they say about the app before anyone signs anything. That costs nothing but a little time.

**Why we're hedging rather than picking**

1. Zoho FSM wins clearly on the two things that structurally hurt you — per-user pricing and vendor sprawl. That's real and it's worth chasing.
2. But it loses badly on the one thing that's actually breaking today, which is a crew that won't open the app. Fixing your second problem while worsening your first isn't progress.
3. The evidence against Zoho's app is thin — 11 iPhone ratings is almost nothing. It would be irresponsible to make a final call on that sample, in either direction.
4. The free tier makes the uncertainty cheap to resolve. When a real test is available for nothing, guessing is the wrong move.

## 10. What to do, in order

1. **Buy nine Jobber seats and change the shared password.** About $261/month. Run it 30 days. This is the only step that's urgent, and it pays for itself in audit trail alone.
2. **Pull two numbers while you wait.** Last year's total card volume, and your average appointments per month. The first settles the QuoteIQ fee question; the second is the only way to price Zoho FSM honestly.
3. **Put two people on Zoho FSM's free tier.** Costs nothing, supports 20 users, and tells you whether the app is really as weak as its rating suggests. Do this alongside the Jobber test, not instead of it.
4. **Ask the crew again at day 30.** If the complaints stopped, you're done — move to Connect or Grow for custom fields and automation and get on with it.
5. **If they didn't stop, get a JobNimbus demo.** Best-rated field app of the five and built for roofers. Ask specifically about the Established plan, since that's the tier with API access, and about total cost at your headcount.
6. **Push more payments to bank transfer either way.** Jobber charges 1% for ACH against 2.9% for cards. On roofing-sized invoices that gap is worth more than most of the decisions in this report.

We'd revisit QuoteIQ in twelve months. If they publish a real two-way API, most of this report needs rewriting in their favor — the product itself is well liked and the pricing is honest. It just can't do the one thing your setup depends on.

## 11. Open questions

None of these change the recommendation, but they should be closed before you sign anything.

1. **Your monthly appointment volume.** Zoho FSM prices by job, not by person. Everything we've said about its cost assumes the entry tier. Without your real number, its figure is a floor and nothing more.
2. **Jobber's user caps per plan.** Jobber's pricing page says extra users are $29/month on any plan. Its help centre says Connect supports up to 5 and Core supports 1. Both can't be right — confirm with your rep before budgeting.
3. **QuoteIQ's 1% convenience fee.** This comes from a reviewer citing QuoteIQ's payment terms, not from QuoteIQ's own pricing page. It's the most consequential number in the cost section and deserves confirming directly. It may also be passable to your customer rather than absorbed.
4. **Whether your crew has ever used Jobber's app properly.** If every complaint came from the shared login, the app has never really been tested. Worth asking before day one, so the before-and-after means something.
5. **Which phones your crew carries.** Every app here rates lower on Android than iPhone. An all-Android crew is having a worse time on any of these, and that's a hardware question, not a vendor one.
6. **Zoho FSM's app on a small sample.** Eleven iPhone ratings is almost nothing. The written complaints back up the low score, but we would not make a final call on that evidence — hence the free-tier trial.
7. **JobNimbus's real total.** Our $11,340 is built from published per-role rates and a guessed staffing mix. Get a written quote at your actual headcount before comparing it seriously.
