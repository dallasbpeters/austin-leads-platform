01 — THE SHORT VERSION

What I found

Your software isn't the problem. The shared login is.

Your crew all sign in with one Jobber account. That saves money, but it breaks things. And I think it's why they say the app doesn't work.

Here are the three things that matter.

  • The shared login is probably breaking the app. Your crew says Jobber's app is unusable. But Jobber's app is one of the best rated on the market. What they describe — getting kicked offline, clock-ins that don't save — is what happens when seven phones share one account.
  • QuoteIQ can't talk to your website. Your site files invoices into Jobber by itself. QuoteIQ can't accept that. Someone would have to type them in by hand again.
  • Zoho is worth a look. You already use Zoho for contacts, quotes and files. Zoho's field service tool charges by job, not by person — so everyone gets their own login for free. But its app is rated poorly, which is a problem when your crew already won't use one.

What I'd do

Buy your crew their own logins. Wait 30 days. Then decide.

Six more Jobber seats cost about $174 a month. That tells you if the app is really the problem. It also gets your records straight and puts you back inside Jobber's rules.

If the complaints stop, you're done. If they don't, look at JobNimbus — not QuoteIQ.

02 — START HERE

The shared login costs more than it saves

You share one login to avoid paying $29 a month per person. That saves you about $348 a year. Here's what it costs you.

  • You can't tell who did what. Every quote and every price change has the same name on it.
  • Time tracking is useless. So is GPS. Both work off the person signed in.
  • Passwords never change. When someone quits, all seven of you would have to switch. It rarely happens.
  • It breaks Jobber's rules. Their terms say accounts are for one person and must not be shared. They could shut the account down.

And it explains the app complaints

Here's what Jobber users complain about in public reviews:

  • "I keep going offline" — has to close and reopen the app several times a day.
  • The app "will tell you it's clocked you in when it has not."
  • "Schedules are not updating live… critical notes are missed."

All three are the same kind of problem. One account signed in on seven phones means each phone keeps kicking the others out. That looks exactly like a broken app.

The main finding

Your crew is right that it doesn't work. They may be wrong about why.

I can't prove this from outside your account. But it fits, and it's cheap to check. And if I'm wrong, you still end up with proper records and working time tracking. You don't lose either way.

04 — COST

What each one costs

They charge in three different ways:

  • Per person — Jobber and JobNimbus. This is what pushed you into sharing logins.
  • Per group of seats — QuoteIQ. Adding people is free until you outgrow your plan.
  • Per job — Zoho. Up to 200 people, at no extra charge.

At seven people the gap between them is small. QuoteIQ's plans jump from 4 seats to 10, so you'd pay for 10 either way.

Watch the card fees

Every one of these takes a cut when a customer pays by card. That cut is bigger than the software bill.

QuoteIQ charges 1% more than Jobber. On $500,000 of card payments that's $5,000 a year. The software only saves you $286. So QuoteIQ ends up costing you roughly $4,700 a year more, not less.

Jobber also charges just 1% for bank transfers instead of 2.9% for cards. On roofing-sized invoices that's worth real money. Worth pushing customers that way whatever you decide.

One thing to keep in mind: all these prices assume everyone has their own login. You're paying about $348 a year right now. Doing this properly costs more no matter which one you pick.

10 — LOOSE ENDS

What I couldn't check

None of this changes my advice, but sort it out before you sign anything.

  1. Your jobs per month. Zoho charges by job. Without your real number, its price on the chart is a floor and nothing more.
  2. QuoteIQ's extra 1%. This came from a reviewer quoting their terms, not from QuoteIQ. It's the most important number in the cost section. Ask them directly.
  3. Jobber's limits per plan. Their pricing page and their help pages disagree about how many people each plan allows. Check with your rep.
  4. Zoho's app score. Eleven ratings is almost nothing. The written reviews back it up, but that's why I'm suggesting a trial rather than a decision.
  5. JobNimbus's real price. My $9,960 assumes one admin, one office person and five in the field. If your seven split differently, so does the price. Get a written quote.
